Privacy policy

Last updated: 10 November 2026

Swipt sends files between your phone and your computer over your own Wi-Fi. It was built to need nothing from you: Swipt collects no personal data. There are no ads, no tracking, no analytics and no account. We (the people who make Swipt) never see your files or anything about you, because nothing passes through us. Swipt has no servers.

This policy covers the Swipt apps for iPhone, Android, Windows and Mac.

How your files travel

  • Files go directly from one of your devices to another over your local network (your Wi-Fi). They never go over the internet and never through a cloud service.
  • Your phone only talks to computers you paired it with, by scanning the QR code on that computer's screen and confirming on the computer.
  • Every transfer is encrypted (HTTPS/TLS). Your phone checks the computer's identity against the code it scanned, so another device can't pretend to be your computer.
  • Received files are saved on the receiving device: on a computer in the folder you chose (Downloads ▸ Swipt by default); on an iPhone in Photos or in the Files app; on Android in Photos or in Downloads ▸ Swipt.

What stays on your devices

Swipt keeps a few things so it works the next time. They stay on your devices and are never sent to us:

  • On your phone: the computers you paired with (their name, network addresses and a random access key), your phone's name, how many of your free files you've used, and whether you unlocked Unlimited Transfers. On iPhone this is in the Keychain. On Android it's in Swipt's own storage and in Google's Block Store on the phone (Swipt sets it to stay on the device, not to be uploaded).
  • On your computer: the phones you paired (name, an ID, a fingerprint of their access key, and the free-file count they last reported), its settings, and the files you received.
  • What your computer learns from your phone: the phone's name, an ID that identifies it to your computers, and how many free files it has left. On Android, the ID is derived from Android's own ID for Swipt on that phone, scrambled so the original can't be read back. All of this stays on your computer.

Purchases

The one-time purchase "Unlimited Transfers" is handled entirely by Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play). We don't receive your payment details or your name. Swipt only asks the store whether you own the purchase, so it can unlock unlimited transfers and restore them on a new phone.

Permissions Swipt asks for

  • Local network: to find your computer on your Wi-Fi and send files to it.
  • Camera: only to scan the pairing QR code. Images are not saved or sent anywhere.
  • Photos: to show your photos and videos so you can pick what to send, and to save photos and videos you receive.
  • Notifications (Windows and Mac): to tell you when files arrive.

You can turn any of these off in your device's settings; Swipt explains what stops working.

Other companies' components

  • Apple and Google handle purchases (see above). On iPhone, if you send a photo that's only stored in iCloud, iOS first downloads it from your iCloud. That's between your phone and Apple.
  • Android QR scanning uses Google's ML Kit, which runs on your phone. Swipt switches off ML Kit's usage reports, so nothing is sent to Google from scanning.

This website

swiptapp.com sets no cookies and has no analytics or tracking. Like any website host, its host (GitHub Pages) may briefly log technical data such as IP addresses to keep the site running and secure.

Children

Swipt doesn't collect data from anyone, including children.
